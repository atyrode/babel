# Babel tunes itself

> **Standing duty, off by default.** This recipe is never part of a default
> selection. It runs when the operator has authorized the personal tuning
> duty — `babel conductor configure --babel-tunes-itself` — or when it is named
> explicitly. Everything it produces is specific to this operator and this
> machine, and stays here: a finding phrased for anyone belongs to the
> product-dimension recipe and must be routed there instead.

## Question

What does Babel hold about this operator's world that the archive no longer
supports, and what does the archive now support that Babel does not hold?

Two directions, one subject. The first is upkeep, and it is the direction that
decays without attention: authorized Reality facts and accepted durable-learning
notes were true when a person authorized them, and the corpus has kept moving
since. A repository was renamed, a service was retired, a convention was
revised, a machine stopped existing, a note's applicability expired. Nothing
deletes itself — Babel's records are append-only and expiry marks rather than
removes — so an unmaintained context store slowly becomes a confident, durable
description of a world that has changed.

The second direction is relevance: what the archive shows this operator
repeatedly needs, which Babel has never been told. A constraint restated in five
sessions and written down nowhere. A predicate whose value is stated in the
corpus and absent from the ledger. A note whose applicability is narrower than
it was recorded as.

Both directions produce the same kind of output: a proposal, addressed to the
person who can authorize it. Nothing here asserts anything. A fact derived from
Babel's own observation can only ever be proposed, and a durable-learning note
stays a proposal until it is accepted — which is precisely why this recipe can
be trusted to look at the operator's own context store at all.

This recipe is descriptive about the record and never about the person. Its
subject is stored context and its evidence, not the operator.

## Inclusion, exclusion, and ambiguity

Include an upkeep candidate when the stored record and the fresher evidence are
both locatable, and say which direction the disagreement runs: the archive
contradicts the fact, the archive has moved past it, or the fact's own freshness
expectation has lapsed with nothing newer to say. The locatable material is:
authorized facts with their subject, predicate, value, valid time, observed time
and derived status — `active`, `stale`, `disputed`, `superseded`; accepted
durable-learning proposals with their destination, statement and stated
applicability; and the archive itself, quoted with locators.

Include a retirement proposal. "This should no longer be in force" is a real
outcome and the one most likely to be skipped, because proposing an addition
feels productive and proposing a retirement feels like undoing work. A stored
fact nobody can support any more costs more than a missing one: it is durable,
authorized, and read as true.

Include scope narrowing. A note recorded as general that the archive shows holds
in one repository is not wrong, it is over-scoped, and the amendment is the
scope rather than the statement.

Include contradictions, and retain both sides. §4.8 makes a contradiction a
dispute rather than a contest one side wins, and the honest output when the
archive and a fact disagree is the dispute with its evidence — not a
replacement value chosen by recency.

Include a relevance candidate only with independent occurrences. A requirement
stated once is a candidate with a count of one and is labelled as such;
repetition across unrelated tasks, restatement after a violation, and
consistency over time are what distinguish standing context from one moment's
instruction.

Exclude anything that would characterize the operator: no inference about
personality, skill, mood, working style as a trait, or intent beyond what was
stated. "This operator prefers X" is acceptable only as shorthand for "X was
stated as a requirement in N independent contexts, with these locators", and the
proposal must carry that evidence.

Exclude anything phrased for anyone. A finding about Babel's output quality, its
guidance, or its retrieval is the product dimension's, and emitting it here
would put a public proposal into this operator's private memory store where
nobody else will ever see it.

Exclude freeform memory. A durable-learning proposal names an enumerated
destination whose own review rules govern it; "remember this" is not a
destination, and a proposal that cannot name one is a finding rather than a
memory.

Exclude a fact this recipe would author rather than propose. Facts require
attributed operator action or a configured trusted source. Analysis proposes;
it never asserts, and it never proposes a value it could not evidence from the
archive.

Ambiguity: absence of recent evidence is not evidence of change. A fact about a
machine nobody has mentioned for four months may be perfectly current — silence
is the normal state of a stable world. The discriminator is positive evidence of
change, or a lapsed freshness expectation the predicate itself declares.
Where neither is present, the correct output is `unverifiable` and, at most, a
question.

Search the frontier before minting. Babel indexes its own outputs and the
operator's recorded review answers; the job document lists the prior records
related to this scope, and the same search is available with
`"scope": "frontier"`. This recipe meets the same context store on every draw
and will re-propose the same amendment unless it looks: where a prior record
already proposes what you would propose, refine it, develop another observation
onto it, or revive it if it came to rest, naming its record id. Where a prior
proposal was declined, read the decline reason before proposing the same thing
again — a re-proposal that does not address the stated reason is noise the
operator has already answered.

## Sorting cues

- a fact whose predicate declares a freshness expectation that has lapsed, with
  no newer observation of the same subject and predicate;
- a fact whose subject the archive shows was renamed, moved, retired, or
  replaced, with the session that shows it;
- an accepted note whose stated applicability names a tool, repository, or
  convention the corpus no longer contains;
- a requirement restated in unrelated sessions and present in no fact and no
  note;
- a note the archive shows being contradicted in practice — followed nowhere
  since it was accepted;
- two authorized facts about one subject and predicate whose values disagree,
  neither superseded;
- a note accepted long ago whose statement the corpus now states more precisely;
- a scope written as global that every occurrence qualifies.

Weak cues: how old a record is on its own, how often a subject appears, and how
confidently the original was worded. Retrieval rank contributes nothing.

## Evidence and counter-evidence

Evidence, quoted with locators:

- the stored record verbatim: fact subject, predicate and value, or the note's
  statement and applicability, with its identifier and its recorded time;
- the fresher archive material, quoted, with session, time and context — the
  quotation is the evidence, and a summary of it is not;
- for a lapsed-freshness claim, the observed time the record carries and the
  expectation the predicate declares;
- for a relevance candidate, each independent statement with its session, time
  and context, and the wording verbatim;
- observable adherence or non-adherence: work that followed a stored note
  without being reminded, or work that did not and drew no objection.

Counter-evidence to seek:

- a newer statement restoring what looked retired — the second half of a
  reversal is easy to miss when the search was built around the first;
- the possibility that the change is local: one repository, one experiment, one
  week, with the general case untouched;
- the possibility that the repetition is one recurring task rather than a
  standing requirement;
- a record that already says this: the amendment may have been proposed and
  declined, or authorized and superseded already;
- selection effects from retrieval: searching for a phrase finds the sessions
  that use it and hides the ones that do not, which for a claim about a
  convention is the whole risk;
- the stable-world explanation: no recent mention is not a change.

Deliberately search for the negation. Having formed a candidate amendment, the
natural search finds its support.

## Temporal and present-reality checks

This recipe is entirely a temporal instrument, so the checks are the work rather
than a final gate:

1. Which is newer — the stored record's observed time, or the archive evidence
   against it? An amendment proposed from evidence older than the record it
   amends is backwards, and the record's own valid time is what settles it.
2. Has the record already moved? Read the fact's status history and the note's
   disposition history before proposing: a fact already `superseded` or `stale`
   needs no proposal, and proposing one wastes the review attention this recipe
   exists to protect.
3. Does the present corpus still contain the context the record depends on? A
   note about a workflow the archive no longer shows is `historical` whatever
   its wording.

Use `still-applicable`, `historical`, `resolved` (already amended or already
recorded), `regressed` (an amended context that has returned), `contradicted`
(the archive states the opposite), and `unverifiable` (the present state is not
observable from the corpus). Weight recent evidence over old, and state the
weighting.

## Classifications and stopping conditions

- `amendment-proposed` — a stored fact or note whose value or wording the
  archive supports changing, with both sides quoted;
- `retirement-proposed` — a stored record nothing supports any more. Include the
  reason it was true when it was authorized;
- `scope-narrowing` — the statement holds, the recorded applicability is wider
  than the evidence;
- `dispute` — two records, or a record and the archive, in direct contradiction,
  both retained, no side chosen;
- `freshness-lapsed` — the record's own expectation has expired with nothing
  newer to say. A proposal to re-observe, not to change the value;
- `relevance-candidate` — standing context the archive supports and Babel does
  not hold, with its independent-occurrence count;
- `insufficient-evidence` — one occurrence, or silence read as change. The
  correct label for most candidates;
- `already-current` — checked and unchanged. Worth recording: it is the result
  that stops the same candidate being re-derived next week.

Stop when both sides are quoted with locators, the record's own status history
has been read, and the direction of the disagreement is stated. Stop before
proposing a relevance amendment on fewer than three independent contexts unless
the statement is an explicit standing instruction. Stop when the honest output
is a question — an unresolved conflict about the operator's own world is exactly
what a Reality Question exists to settle, and asking is cheaper and more
accurate than inferring.

Never authorize. Every output here is proposed until the operator says
otherwise, and a run that behaved as though its own observation could authorize
a fact would have broken the boundary that makes this recipe safe to point at
the operator's context store.

## Cross-session synthesis keys

Group by: the stored record's subject; the predicate or destination; the
direction of the disagreement — added, amended, narrowed, retired, disputed;
the archive evidence's own recency; and the scope the amendment claims.

Recurrence is the promotion criterion for the relevance half and the
disqualifier for the upkeep half. A standing requirement is defined by
independent contexts, so count them and never count one session twice. An
upkeep candidate needs no recurrence at all — one credible piece of fresher
evidence is enough to propose re-observation — but a candidate that recurs
across draws without ever being authorized is a finding about the proposal, not
about the record: it has been answered by silence or declined, and re-proposing
it is the failure mode.

Synthesis should cross-reference the durable-operator-model lens, whose subject
is the same evidence for a different purpose: that lens asks which instructions
are durable, and this recipe asks what Babel should hold as a result. A relevance
candidate here that the lens has already recorded as an observation should be
emitted against that record rather than beside it.

## Capability needs

- `corpus-search` is required and is the only capability this recipe needs. Both
  halves are retrieval: the upkeep half searches for evidence newer than a
  stored record, and the relevance half counts independent occurrences. The most
  important search is still the one that looks for the negation.
- No `repo-read`: a claim about a repository's present state belongs to a lens
  that can evidence it against a pinned snapshot, and an amendment proposed from
  a repository reading Babel did not take would be a fact this recipe invented.
  Where a candidate needs a snapshot, it says so and stops.
- No `sandbox-exec` and no `public-research`: the evidence is this operator's
  own archive.

Dispositions this recipe emits: `propose-reality-fact` for an amendment,
retirement, dispute or re-observation of a Reality fact; `store-memory` for a
durable-learning proposal, naming an enumerated destination;
`ask-question` where the record cannot settle the answer; and
`develop-further` where a candidate needs another pass. It emits no
`draft-issue`: a draft issue is public, phrased for anyone, and belongs to the
product dimension, which is a different toggle and a different audience.

## Known failure modes

- **Silence read as change.** The defining hazard of the upkeep half. A stable
  world produces no evidence, and a recipe that proposes retirement for
  everything unmentioned would strip the context store on its first run.
- **Characterizing the operator.** Out of bounds. The subject is stored context
  and its evidence.
- **Over-generalizing an instruction.** A local imperative promoted to standing
  context misfires in every later run and is expensive to notice.
- **Resolving a contradiction by recency.** Two disagreeing records are evidence
  about scope; picking the newer destroys that evidence and authorizes a value
  nobody chose.
- **Proposing a value the archive does not state.** The amendment must be
  quotable. An inferred value in a durable fact is the worst possible place for
  a guess.
- **Re-proposing what was declined.** The decline reason is in the record. A
  proposal that does not address it is a proposal the operator has already
  answered, arriving again.
- **Crossing the dimension line.** A product finding stored as this operator's
  memory is a public proposal buried where nobody will read it.
- **Confusing frequency with force.** A prohibition stated once may bind harder
  than a preference stated ten times.
- **Claiming authority.** Nothing here asserts. Analysis proposes, an operator
  authorizes, and the two are different records written by different hands.
- **Compliance as correctness.** A fully evidenced amendment can still be a
  misreading of a scope.

## Examples

A useful `retirement-proposed` observation: an authorized fact records a
service's deployment host, observed eleven months ago. Three sessions in the
last two months show the same service addressed at a different host, one of them
quoting a migration the operator performed; no session since mentions the
recorded host at all. Both sides are quoted with locators. Classification
`retirement-proposed` with a superseding value stated as a proposal, temporal
status `contradicted`, and the disposition is a `propose-reality-fact` that an
operator must authorize before anything changes.

A useful `freshness-lapsed` observation: a fact whose predicate declares a
ninety-day freshness expectation carries an observed time seven months old, and
the corpus contains nothing newer about that subject and predicate — no
contradiction, no confirmation. The proposal is to re-observe, not to change the
value, and the observation says so explicitly: this is the case where a
confident amendment would be an invention. Classification `freshness-lapsed`,
temporal status `unverifiable`.

A useful `scope-narrowing` observation: an accepted durable-learning note states
a verification requirement generally. Nine occurrences across the archive
qualify it to one repository, and two sessions in a second repository did the
opposite with no objection. Classification `scope-narrowing`; the proposal
amends the applicability and leaves the statement alone.

A candidate correctly rejected: a fact about a machine that has not appeared in
the corpus for five months looked retired. The counter-evidence found no
positive evidence of any change and a predicate that declares no freshness
expectation, so the silence supports nothing. Classification
`insufficient-evidence`, recorded with its reasoning so the next draw does not
re-derive it.

An error to avoid: "the operator has become more tolerant of unverified claims;
relax the verification note". It characterizes a person, infers a trend from
absence, and proposes weakening stored context on the strength of it. The valid
version would quote the sessions, count the independent contexts, state the
scope, and — finding no positive evidence — stop at a question.
